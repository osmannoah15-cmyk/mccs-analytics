What these measures mean
Margin rate
Gross margin as a share of revenue. How much of every dollar taken in is kept after the cost of goods.
Trend
Revenue in the most recent half of the period against the earlier half. Positive means the line is growing.
Average transaction
Revenue divided by transactions. What a patron spends per visit.
Stock cover
How many days of selling the stock on hand would cover at the current rate. Lower means leaner. Too low risks running out, too high ties up cash in inventory that is not selling. Only lines that hold physical stock have this.
Stock turns
How many times a year the stock is sold through and replaced. Six turns means roughly two months of stock on the shelf at any time. Higher generally means capital is working harder.
Promotion ROI
Return on marketing spend for that line. Zero is break-even, so a negative figure means the promotions cost more than the margin they produced.
Recommendation rule: scale when the trend exceeds 6% and margin rate exceeds 25%, review when the trend is negative or margin rate falls below 15%. Every input sits in the row, so the call can be challenged rather than taken on faith.

How each lever works
Patron demand
More or fewer patrons buying at the same prices. Moves revenue, and margin follows at the current rate.
Promotion budget
Also moves revenue, at the return per dollar these campaigns actually achieved. The cost of the extra spend is subtracted, so more spend only helps if the return beats it.
Gross margin rate
Pricing or cost of goods. Changes what is kept from revenue, not revenue itself.
Move spend out of losing campaigns
Total spend is unchanged. Money in campaigns that do not return their cost is redirected to the best performing channel.
